What Is a Stroke?

When Blood Flow to the Brain Is Blocked or Reduced

A stroke occurs when blood flow to the brain is blocked or reduced, depriving brain tissue of oxygen. This can result in permanent brain damage, disability, or even death if not treated immediately. There are two primary types of stroke:

Ischemic Stroke — caused by a blood clot blocking an artery.

Hemorrhagic Stroke — caused by a ruptured blood vessel in the brain.

Symptoms of a stroke can include:

  • Sudden numbness or weakness, especially on one side of the body
  • Confusion or trouble speaking
  • Vision problems in one or both eyes
  • Dizziness or loss of coordination
  • Severe headache with no known cause

When these warning signs appear, doctors must act fast. Failure to diagnose or properly treat a stroke can lead to irreversible harm — and that's when we step in to hold negligent medical providers accountable.

Often there are early warning signs, known as a transient ischemic attack or TIA, that present before a devastating stroke — numbness in the face, arm, or leg, usually on one side of the body; difficulty speaking or understanding speech; vision problems; dizziness or loss of balance; confusion or difficulty thinking. These symptoms typically last for a few minutes to a few hours and then resolve on their own. If a TIA is properly diagnosed and treated, a larger and more devastating stroke, resulting in lifelong disability, can be prevented.

How Negligence Happens

You Might Be Entitled to Compensation

We represent individuals and families who have suffered because of:

Failure to Diagnose a Stroke

Doctors and emergency room staff must recognize stroke symptoms immediately. If your stroke was dismissed as something minor — or not diagnosed at all — you may have been denied life-saving treatment.

Improper Stroke Treatment

If your medical team failed to administer the proper medications (like tPA or dual anti-platelet therapy), provide necessary mechanical or surgical intervention (like a thrombectomy), follow proper stroke protocols, or properly manage blood pressure, they may be liable for medical negligence.

Overlooked Stroke Warning Signs

Doctors and nurses must recognize stroke symptoms, even in younger patients or those without obvious risk factors. If a stroke should have been suspected but was ignored or mistaken for another condition, the consequences can be tragic.
